Medicare Reimbursement Cuts 2026: What Every NY Provider Must Do Right Now to Protect Revenue

12 min read By Xpert Billing Services Westchester · Dutchess · Rockland · NYC · Nassau

Medicare physician pay has fallen 33% in real terms since 2001. The 2026 CMS fee schedule brings specialty cuts, a controversial efficiency adjustment, and rising Medicare Advantage denials that are quietly draining provider revenue. Here is exactly what is happening — and what you can do about it.

Critical Alert for NY Providers — October 2026

Medicare physician reimbursement has declined 33% in real terms since 2001 (AMA). The 2026 CMS fee schedule finalizes a -2.5% efficiency adjustment on top of specialty cuts, while Medicare Advantage plans denied 7.7% of all prior authorization requests in 2025. NY practices cannot afford to leave revenue on the table.

What Is Actually Happening With Medicare in 2026

On October 31, 2025, CMS released the final 2026 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule — and while the headline shows a base rate increase of 3.26%, the full picture is far more damaging for many specialties and practice types.

After five consecutive years of payment cuts, Congress stepped in through the "One Big Beautiful Bill" with a one-time 2.5% increase. But CMS simultaneously finalized a -2.5% efficiency adjustment applying to most non-time-based services — effectively canceling the Congressional raise for most providers before it took effect.

The AMA was direct: adjusted for inflation in practice costs, Medicare reimbursement for physician services has declined 33% from 2001 to 2026. That is not a rounding error. That is a structural collapse in physician payment building for a quarter century — and 2026 accelerates it.

The efficiency adjustment is based on CMS's assumption that technology improvements have made physicians more productive. Physician groups called the reasoning flawed and the methodology unsound. CMS finalized it anyway.

The site-neutrality changes also reduce what Medicare pays physicians practicing in hospitals versus their own offices — an attempt to level the playing field between hospital systems and independent practices. Facility-based payments drop 7%; office-based care rises 4%. Independent NY practices should pay close attention to this shift.

The Medicare Advantage Denial Crisis — Worse Than Ever in 2026

Medicare Advantage enrollment now covers more than half of all Medicare beneficiaries — and for providers, that means dealing with a system built around prior authorization, automated downcoding, and claim denials as a profit mechanism.

In MGMA's 2026 Regulatory Burden Report, three of the top five administrative burdens named by practices are tied directly to Medicare Advantage: prior authorization, claim denials, and automatic downcoding. Among practices seeing patients shift into MA, 79% said the move has hurt their operations.

⚠️ What the Data Shows About Medicare Advantage in 2026

  • MA plans made nearly 53 million prior authorization determinations in 2024, denying 7.7% in full or in part
  • Medicare Advantage denials result in a 7% net reduction in provider MA revenue — even after successful appeals
  • OIG found that among appealed SNF prior auth denials, 95% were overturned in the provider's favor — meaning initial denials were largely inappropriate
  • CMS began accelerating RADV (Risk Adjustment Data Validation) audits in spring 2026, adding further compliance burden
  • UnitedHealthcare pledged to eliminate 30% of prior auth requirements by end of 2026 — but analysts say the real-world impact on denial rates will be minimal

The OIG data is particularly striking: when providers actually appeal Medicare Advantage denials, they win 95% of the time. That means the vast majority of denials being accepted without appeal represent pure lost revenue — revenue your practice earned and is legally owed.

Most practices do not have the staffing or expertise to systematically appeal every denial. That is where the money quietly walks out the door.

What This Means for NY Providers Specifically

New York providers face a compounded challenge. The state's high cost of living means practice expenses — rent, staff salaries, malpractice insurance — have risen far faster than the national average, while Medicare reimbursement has declined in real terms just like everywhere else.

Practices across Westchester, Dutchess, Rockland, Nassau County, and New York City are contending with:

  • Rising overhead costs against declining Medicare reimbursement rates
  • Increasing Medicare Advantage enrollment shifting patients away from traditional Medicare's simpler claims process
  • More complex prior authorization requirements eating into staff time and focus
  • A growing gap between what insurers pay and what it actually costs to run a practice
  • Pressure on smaller independent practices to consolidate with hospital systems just to survive financially

The Provider Reimbursement Stability Act (PRSA), introduced in Congress in March 2026, would address some of these issues — but legislative timelines are unpredictable, and practices cannot wait for Washington to fix a problem that is actively harming revenue right now.

7 Concrete Steps NY Providers Can Take Right Now to Protect Revenue

Despite the challenging landscape, there are real, proven strategies that protect and grow practice revenue in 2026. Here is what works.

1. Audit Your Current Denial Rate — The Data Will Surprise You

Most practices do not know their actual denial rate or what it is costing them annually. Pull a 90-day report of all denied claims by payer. For most practices, even a 5% reduction in denials translates to tens of thousands of dollars recovered per year.

2. Build a Systematic Appeal Process for Every Medicare Advantage Denial

Given that 95% of appealed denials are overturned, every unchallenged denial is money left on the table. Practices need a documented process for reviewing, correcting, and resubmitting denied claims within payer deadlines. This alone can recover 3–7% of MA revenue currently being written off.

3. Verify Eligibility and Prior Auth Status Before Every Encounter

The single most preventable source of claim denials is submitting a claim for a service that needed prior authorization and did not get it. Real-time eligibility verification at the time of scheduling — not the day of the appointment — catches most of these before they become denials.

4. Review Your Payer Mix and Renegotiate Commercial Contracts

As Medicare rates decline, commercial payer contracts become proportionally more important. Many NY practices have not renegotiated commercial rates in years. A rate increase of even 3–5% across your commercial contracts can offset Medicare cuts significantly.

5. Shift Services to Nonfacility Settings Where Possible

The 2026 site-neutrality changes cut facility-based payments by 7% while increasing nonfacility office-based care by 4%. For services that can be performed in either setting, moving them to your office rather than a hospital or ASC may significantly improve reimbursement under the new fee schedule.

6. Explore Alternative Payment Models (APMs)

APM participants receive a higher conversion factor (3.77% vs 3.26%) and partial insulation from the efficiency adjustment. If your practice volume and patient mix qualifies, participation in an APM could meaningfully improve your Medicare payment rates. Discuss with your billing team and accountant whether this makes sense for your practice.

7. Outsource Billing to Specialists Who Track These Changes Full-Time

The 2026 fee schedule changes are complex. Coding updates, site-of-service differentials, the efficiency adjustment, MIPS performance — keeping up with all of it while running a practice is genuinely difficult. Specialized medical billing companies track these changes as their primary job, resulting in consistently higher clean claim rates, fewer denials, faster payment, and more recovered revenue.
